Eight ways to
reveal an image

Each section uses a genuinely different mechanism — clip-path, SVG filters, 3D transforms, CSS masks and channel separation. They fire once on scroll; hit Replay on any of them to run it again.

Scroll
01

Aperture

A circular clip opens from the centre while the photograph counter-zooms from 1.45× down to 1×. The opposing motion is what sells the depth — the frame grows outward as the subject settles back.

02

Ink Bleed

A soft radial mask grows outward while an SVG turbulence filter distorts the edge and settles to zero. The boundary crawls and feathers like ink soaking into paper rather than a clean geometric edge.

03

Hinge

The whole frame is a door hinged on its left edge, swinging from 88° edge-on to flat. A gradient shade rides on top and lifts as it turns, so the surface catches light the way a real panel would.

04

Shatter

The frame is cut into twenty-four irregular triangles, each a full copy of the image clipped to its own polygon. They fly in from scattered directions, spinning, and lock together into a single surface.

05

Fly-Through

The image starts 1400px back in Z-space, tilted off-axis and thrown out of focus. It travels toward the viewer and squares up, the blur clearing as it arrives — depth of field doing the work rather than a mask.

06

Ripple

A repeating radial mask of 28px concentric rings, whose opaque portion widens from nothing to full. The picture fills in as rings spreading out from the centre, like the surface of water settling.

07

Fabric Drop

The image falls from above pinned at its top edge, overshoots, and a skew ripples through it on an elastic ease that decays to nothing — the way a sheet of cloth settles after you let go of it.

08

Chromatic Split

Three copies of the image, each stripped to a single colour channel and blended back with screen. They start pulled apart and rotated, then converge — the picture resolving out of its own red, green and blue as a lens correcting.
